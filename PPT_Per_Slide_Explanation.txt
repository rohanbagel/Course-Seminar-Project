[What to Say (Spoken Script)]:
"To appreciate the significance of this architecture, we must examine the paradigm shift in financial automation.
Traditionally, the industry relied on Robotic Process Automation (RPA) and hardcoded regex scripts. While RPA was fast for standardized forms, it was completely brittle: a minor shift in a prospectus table’s column sequence or font layout caused the parser to fail entirely.

Next came supervised classifiers like FinBERT, which could predict sentiment on isolated sentences but possessed zero multi-step logical reasoning capabilities.

Modern Cognitive AI Agents represent Stage 5 of this evolution. Powered by foundation models equipped with the ReAct paradigm—Reasoning plus Acting—an agent perceives an unstructured input, plans a sequence of actions, invokes external tools like APIs and math engines, observes the tool output, and reflects before responding. In our platform, we extend this from a single agent into an ensemble committee that debates hypotheses collaboratively."

[What to Say (Spoken Script)]:

In 2019, Dogu Araci introduced FinBERT, fine-tuning BERT on 10-K corporate filings. While it established a benchmark for financial sentiment, it was strictly a sentence-level classifier incapable of generative synthesis.

In 2023, Wu et al. developed BloombergGPT, a 50-billion-parameter dense model trained on 363 billion financial tokens. Despite its domain scale, it remains closed-source, computationally prohibitive, and cannot interact dynamically with external web APIs or database tools.

Yang et al. democratized the space with FinGPT, using low-rank adaptation on open-source LLMs. However, like most standalone foundation models, FinGPT suffers from single-agent hallucinations and severe arithmetic limitations when performing multi-step financial math.

The breakthrough came with Yao et al.'s ReAct framework, which paired reasoning traces with external tool actions, and subsequent multi-agent debate works like FinCon and FinMem, which proved that an adversarial committee of agents substantially reduces hallucinations compared to single-agent prompting."

SLIDE 7: 3. LITERATURE SURVEY: ReAct LOOP & HYBRID DENSE-SPARSE RAG
========================================================================================
[Visual Elements on Slide]:
- Visual Architecture diagram:
  * Left: ReAct Interleaved Cycle (Thought -> Action [Tool Call] -> Observation -> Final Answer).
  * Right: Hybrid RAG Pipeline (BGE-Large Dense Vector Embeddings + BM25 Sparse Keyword Inverted Index fused via Reciprocal Rank Fusion [RRF]).

[What to Say (Spoken Script)]:
"Slide 7 illustrates the two technical cornerstones identified in our survey.
On the left is the ReAct loop: rather than hallucinating corporate figures, the agent outputs a thought, triggers a tool execution—such as querying historical P/E multiples or executing a formula—and conditions its subsequent thought on the verified observation.

On the right is our solution to the notorious 'Lost-in-the-Middle' phenomenon. In a 600-page prospectus, critical legal risks and related-party footnotes are frequently buried in the middle pages. Standard dense vector embeddings often miss specific statutory terms or clause numbers. 
Therefore, we implement a Hybrid Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline. We run dense semantic retrieval using BGE-Large alongside BM25 sparse keyword matching, fusing the ranked results using Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF). This guarantees that both high-level semantic themes and exact statutory clause references are retrieved with high recall."

[Potential Evaluator Question]:
Q: "What is Reciprocal Rank Fusion and why not just use cosine similarity?"
A: "Cosine similarity measures vector angle in semantic space, which works well for concepts but struggles with exact alphanumeric strings like legal clause numbers or statutory section codes. RRF combines the rank orders from both BM25 keyword search and dense vector search: RRF Score = 1 / (60 + Rank_Dense) + 1 / (60 + Rank_Sparse). This balances conceptual relevance with precise term matching."

[What to Say (Spoken Script)]:

Gap 1 is Multimodal Prospectus Complexity. DRHPs contain restated financial statements spanning several pages with merged headers, accounting footnotes, and annexures that standard OCR tools flatten into unreadable text chunks.
Gap 2 is the Lost-in-the-Middle Vulnerability. Even models with 128k token context windows suffer severe attention degradation in the middle 60% of long contexts, where companies routinely bury material litigation and statutory caveats.
Gap 3 is the Numerical Hallucination Problem. Language models are probabilistic token predictors, not calculators. Permitting an LLM to calculate ratios directly results in unpredictable, untrusted outputs.
Gap 4 is Retail Information Asymmetry. Retail investors possess neither the 20 hours required to audit each prospectus nor access to institutional quantitative terminals, leading to misinformed speculation during market frenzies."

[What to Say (Spoken Script)]:

First is the OFS Dilution Ratio. When the OFS ratio exceeds 50%, the majority of public funds exit to existing private equity sponsors rather than funding company growth capex. In extreme cases like Hyundai Motor India, an OFS ratio of 100% means zero rupees flow into the Indian operating entity.

Second is the renowned Beneish 8-Variable M-Score model. Developed by Prof. Messod Beneish, this econometric model calculates eight indices tracking revenue acceleration (DSRI), gross margin decay (GMI), non-current asset shifts (AQI), depreciation deceleration (DEPI), and total accruals relative to total assets (TATA). If the calculated M-Score exceeds negative 1.78, the company exhibits a high statistical probability of earnings manipulation.

Third is our Listing Spike Probability Cone, which combines a logistic probability distribution with subscription elasticity to compute a three-point confidence bound: Bearish P10, Expected P50, and Bullish P90 listing price bands."

[Potential Evaluator Question]:
Q: "Why is TATA (Total Accruals to Total Assets) weighted so heavily (+4.037) in the Beneish equation?"
A: "TATA measures the gap between accounting Net Income and actual Cash Flow from Operations: (Net Income - CFO) / Total Assets. When net income is positive but operating cash flow is deeply negative, it indicates that profits exist only on paper through aggressive accruals. Beneish's empirical regression assigned it the highest weight because cash flow divergence is the single strongest predictor of accounting fraud."

           TOP 10 COMMON VIVA / EVALUATION DEFENSE QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Q1: "Why do you use multiple agents instead of a single prompt asking GPT-4 or Claude to analyze everything?"
